Preparing for the Fall Horizon.

President’s Message

Dear GSOLE Members,

As most everybody knows, July ended with the shape of writing studies in flux. On July 24, 2026, the National Council of Teacher of English (NCTE) voted to de-charter the Conference on College Composition and Communication (CCCC)–the organizational home for scholars and practitioners in writing studies and its adjacent disciplines and sub-disciplines. Personally, I have been attending the Meeting of the CCCC since 1998 (Phoenix, AZ), and, in the meantime, I have served on two committees, chaired a SIG, chaired a Standing Group, facilitated multiple workshops, and presented on panels. But most importantly, I attended brilliant and thought-provoking panels that have shaped the instructor and scholar that I am, especially the ones that you all presented. And I have gotten to know many of you while eating tacos in Houston, dining at a chic cafe in Portland, conquering large burritos in Spokane, negotiating a table of almost twenty GSOLE members at a Baltimore Cheesecake Factory, among many social outings. So whether you have been in writing studies for “a minute” or you just started your career, I know you have your own memories and feelings about the CCCC. And I encourage you to share them with your local colleagues, your family, and other GSOLE members. We need this moment of catharsis.

We also need to start thinking about what is next. First, let me report that the GSOLE Executive Board has voted to disaffiliate from the NCTE primarily because their process left many scholars and practitioners without recourse and answers to the future of the CCCC and the resources it offered, some of which our members have produced and were currently producing. We also want to encourage you all to let your local writing studies peers know that GSOLE is here to be an academic home to scholars and practitioners who teach online or use online teaching strategies (e.g., using an Learning Management System, like Canvas). We invite them into GSOLE to help us shape the future of our organization as we chart our path primarily guided by the principles of accessibility and inclusivity (see GSOLE’s first principle). I want to encourage you–current members–to be involved in the process of shaping this new chapter of the field. You too can participate in GSOLE in many different ways–from attending the monthly Cyber Salons to being a conference volunteer to stepping into leadership roles. Outside of GSOLE, there are also several ideation meetings; many are regional, but one is specifically for online instructors.

Our field has lost a significant cornerstone of its foundation, but we are strong and will use this opportunity to create something that aligns more with college writing studies’ needs and vision. I look forward to working with you.

Best,
Kevin DePew, GSOLE President
kdepew@odu.edu (email me if you want to volunteer for GSOLE positions)

🎙️ Call for Presenters

2026–2027 GSOLE Webinar Series

Theme: “Durable Reading Practices in Online Instruction”

Proposals Due August 16, 2026

The Global Society of Online Literacy Educators (GSOLE), an organization dedicated to supporting hybrid and online teacher-scholars and tutors, invites both GSOLE members and non-members to submit proposals for this year’s series: “Durable Reading Practices in Online Instruction.”

GSOLE webinars are designed to spark conversation, provide professional development, share ideas, and build community. Webinars are recorded and made available in our online archive for members, and leaders are strongly encouraged to transform their webinar topics into Research in Online Literacy Education (ROLE) or OLOR Effective Practices publications.

⏱️ Webinar Format

  • • Led by 1–3 presenters
  • • 60 minutes long (45-minute presentation + 15 minutes discussion)
  • • Combines theory and practice in online, hybrid, & digital spaces
  • • Interactive presentations that actively engage attendees

📋 Proposal Information Required

  • • All presenter names, emails, & institutional affiliations
  • • A tentative webinar title & preferred webinar months
  • • 500-word proposal detailing coverage, relation to theme, interactive components, and 4–5 participant outcomes

💡 Potential Webinar Topics:

Which literacy practices outlast their tools, and how can we teach the critical skills and knowledge that move with readers and learners? Suggestions include:

  • • Durable literacy practices for the current moment
  • • Deep reading strategies & making reading visible
  • • Reading as it intersects with and supports writing
  • • Teaching reading accessibly & tools that encourage accessibility
  • • Teaching strategies & assignments for short- and long-form reading
  • • Assessing reading and student success
  • • Reading in writing centers, WPA, and WAC
  • • Deep reading/complex writing tools (Perusall, Hypothesis, LMS)
  • • Tracking reading processes & considering surveillance effects
  • • AI integration, AI literacy, & using AI to develop literacy skills

Submit your proposal by Sunday, August 16, 2026

The webinar committee provides technical support and prep assistance for accepted presenters.

Leadership Opportunity

New Position: GSOLE Archivist

GSOLE has created an archivist position to help organize and store its content for efficient future retrieval. This is a great position for anyone who wants to apply the archival research methods they have studied. The job description is as follows:

As an organization that operates predominantly virtually, GSOLE has generated a lot of online artifacts associated with its daily operations, and the resources it provides on the Online Literacy Educator community (e.g., conference, webinars, publications, certification course). As a result, we are looking for a head archivist who will take on the following responsibilities…

📋 Key Responsibilities:

  • • Recruit members of Archive Committee
  • • Create a proposal for organizing, displaying, retrieving and sunsetting existing GSOLE artifacts
  • • Collaborate with leadership on formalized policy and procedure for the on-going creation, display, and retrieval of future artifacts
  • • Work with the Content Curator on design and retrieval of artifacts from the GSOLE website
  • • Make videos of presenters’ sessions available during the GSOLE conference
  • • Coordinate with presenters featured in videos on the long-term storage of GSOLE conference session and webinar videos
  • • Attend Executive Board meetings (monthly)

Recommended Proposal Topics:
