Activity 3: Human vs. Data

Physical Networking & AI Limits

Find a peer sitting nearby. Swap stories, but bypass generic introductions. Ask context-rich questions focused on unique subjective perspectives.

Try asking these questions:
  • "What specialized knowledge or skill do you hold that AI couldn't replicate?"
  • "What is something you genuinely love that recommender systems consistently misjudge?"
Tip: Need help designing prompts? Query Gemini: "Give me three qualitative, human-centric icebreaker questions..."

Collect two physical sticky notes. Write your partner's name on Note 1, and write a 3-word summation of their distinct human trait on Note 2. Post them to our designated classroom whiteboard, arranging them in clusters based on themes.

Step back and examine our physical dataset. Where do patterns cross? What unique individual features stand out? Look closely for emergent clusters: highly specific hobbies, complex empathy markers, or surprising artistic tastes.
If an algorithmic script evaluated our class data roster, what would it register? It would log dry categories: age range, majors, postal codes. What qualitative context is fully lost in plain parameters? What essential parts of who we are remain absent from simple databases?
